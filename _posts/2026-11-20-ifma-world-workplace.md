Grateful for the opportunity to attend **IFMA World Workplace 2026** in Anaheim, California — one of the premier global gatherings at the intersection of facility management, sustainable infrastructure, and the future of the built environment.

## Scholarship Recognition

It was a humbling moment to be featured on the main stage as a recipient of the **George Graves Scholarship** and the **IFMA Houston Chapter Scholarship**, both awarded through the [IFMA Foundation](https://www.ifmafoundation.org/). I am deeply grateful to these organizations for their generous support and investment in the next generation of professionals.

## Ignite Competition

One of the most energizing experiences at the conference was participating in the **Ignite competition** — a fast-paced presentation format where each speaker communicates their ideas to a live audience. It was a great opportunity to sharpen my ability to convey complex research concepts clearly and concisely to an interdisciplinary crowd.

## Reflections

One of the highlights of this conference was witnessing how research and industry practice can come together to address real-world challenges — from energy-efficient facilities to resilient infrastructure systems. These conversations directly resonate with my doctoral research at the [Beck Research Group](https://beck.cive.uh.edu/) at the University of Houston, where I focus on probabilistic fragility and resilience assessment of electric power distribution networks under wind hazards.

I am also deeply grateful to my advisor, **Dr. Abigail L. Beck**, for her continued guidance, encouragement, and support in pursuing opportunities that broaden my research and professional development.

I leave Anaheim inspired, grateful, and excited to apply these insights as I continue my doctoral research.
